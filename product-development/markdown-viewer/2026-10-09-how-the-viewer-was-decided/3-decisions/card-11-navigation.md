**Cost lines:**
- Outline pane: none exists; fold-all leaves the headings as contents in the reading window (capability note, "What the viewer does"). A pane is built new; the capability note gives no cost.
- Jump keys or a heading list: the capability note names neither among what the viewer does (it names F5, Ctrl-O, heading clicks and #anchor links), so either is built new and is not costed.
- Folding: exists, and reload keeps the folds by heading title (capability note).
- The rendering and the find bar are the operator's own modules (streamdoc 1.3, tkdown 2.1 in teatotal), so a pane that reads the heading regions is work by the same hands (capability note, "The program").

**Opposite:** Why not folding alone, which the program has today and costs nothing? Twenty-eight weighted rows of 496 claim folding at all and none of the 86 reader rivals sells it without an outline; the leading sentences each promise "a contents outline" in those words; and "fold" does not appear in what searchers are offered, though that was not tested by a capture of its own (SD-13). Folding has one thing an outline pane lacks, that the folded headings are the contents in the reading window itself, and the find bar opening the section a hit sits in is built on it (card 12). Against an outline as such: the largest reader by installs, glow (59,634 Homebrew installs a year, N1), claims no way around a document at all, so an outline is what the documentation and AI readers are sold, not what every reader's take-up needs.
