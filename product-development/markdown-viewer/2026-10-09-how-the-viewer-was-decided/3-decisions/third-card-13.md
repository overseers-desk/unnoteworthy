### Boundary tests

**Opposite.** Content leaving the device is the stated posture of 13/496, and of 1 reader in 86 (grip). It has buyers: grip draws 30,167 PyPI downloads a month while unreleased for three years (N4). They are buyers of an exact GitHub rendering, not of a reader. The opposite of 13c, telemetry sent, is a quarter of the App Store's declarations (25/134).

**Further.** Beyond "local": no network access at all (Just a Markdown Viewer), and remote images off until the reader asks. Folio ("A single switch stops documents from loading images from the web"), Markdown Peek ("Images from the web stay off until you choose to show them") and Marklet ("unless you enable Load Remote Images") do this: 3 of 86 readers. Marked QL goes the other way and embeds remote images.

**Joint.** Decided with card 8. Diagrams rendered by a server (ThisIs-Developer: "Diagram source can be sent to PlantUML, Kroki") break 13b's landing, so math and diagrams must be rendered on the machine for both landings to stand together. Decided with card 15: "share or send from the product" (81/496) sends content by the buyer's act, which a "nothing leaves" sentence must allow for. Decided with card 4: an update check uses the network ("updates itself, stated" 58/496, V12(b)), and 13a's first option excludes it.

**Buyer.** No live buyer is held. N6 read the review feeds of five App Store apps, none of them a reader, and no review there is about data leaving; the complaints are about paying and lost edits. The landing predicts that the same feed for Read.md (84 ratings, local stated, the one reader in the store's most-rated eleven) holds reviews that mention files staying local or reading offline. A feed with none would say the claim is printed for the seller's sake, not the buyer's.

**Cost lines:** not read by this clerk; the holder of the capability record adds them beside these landings. Nothing in the open files suggests a local reader cannot deliver 13b's or 13c's landing. The one flag is joint: if card 8 lands on math and diagrams, rendering them without a server.
