### Boundary tests

**Opposite:** Why not charge, as 8 paid-before-use and 12 freemium reader rivals do? They sell through the Mac App Store or a licence checkout (finding 9: in-app and store purchase are 56 and 29 of 145 STORE-NS rows). This offering has neither, and among the rows shaped like it a sale is taken by no one (finding 9: SHAPE, no sale taken 51, sponsorship 35, store purchase 2, nothing else). Charging would also put it into the cluster of complaints the reviews hold (N6).

**Further:** Why not go further than free, and pay the buyer back in some way, or say "free forever" as a promise? "Free forever" appears on the pages (R81 telari, R98 MarkText, R120 mdhero), and telari's page says in the same breath that it "is paid software — free during the public beta". A promise of time answers a different question, "will it still exist next year" (questions file, After), not this card's. No figure in the run separates "free" from "free forever".

**Joint:** With Card 4: the unit is a copy with no licence, so free is the only price the unit can carry without a licence mechanism. With Card 6: a named open-source licence answers "can I use it at work" and "what does it cost" in one line for the buyer who reads the licence when the price is silent. With Card 3: a Homebrew formula page has no price field, so on that surface free is said by the licence or not at all.

**Buyer:** No live buyer is held. Prediction against named rivals: if free without a tier is what the reader market rewards, the two free Mac App Store readers first released in 2026 (Just a Markdown Viewer, R14, 2026-08-04; Marklens, R38, 2026-06-10) should gather ratings at a pace not below the freemium ones of the same year (MD Flow, R48, 2026-04-21; Read.md, R73, 2026-03-25) on a second N6 read.
