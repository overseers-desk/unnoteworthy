Read `briefs/standing-brief-block.md` first, then `briefs/venue-situation.md`, then `/usr/local/src/aesop/pmf-sage/sage-A-adjudicate.md`, then `3-decisions/questions.md`, then `3-decisions/strike-list.md`, then `0-comparables/shape-note.md`, then `0-comparables/findings.md`, then `0-comparables/taxonomy.md`, then `1-competitions/rival-register.md`, then `1-competitions/search-demand-findings.md`, then `1-competitions/distributor-notes.md`, then `1-competitions/sales-record.md`, then `1-competitions/capability-note.md`, then `3-decisions/third-card-1.md`, then `3-decisions/third-card-0.md`. The roster of the operator's contacts is opened to you by the launch message, as a roster and nothing else.

The second owner gate is one ruling round: the buyer card (card 1) and every card that follows it. The method's card-set section says how the following cards are found: a seller's sentence is written for each leading buyer on the buyer card, and the parameters those sentences contain get cards. Your job is those sentences and that list.

The leading buyers on card 1 are three. Its Recommended line withholds between developers and writers together as the documentation reader (RULED strike-list:developers-and-coders, RULED strike-list:writers-and-authors), 19 of the 204 comparables shaped like the operator, and developers and coders without writers (RULED strike-list:developers-and-coders), 9 of 204. The market-only reading in `3-decisions/third-card-1.md` withholds between the documentation reader and readers of what AI agents write (RULED strike-list:readers-of-what-ai-agents-write), 8 of 86 reader rivals. Card 0 recommends that the offering stays a single program for all three, by 144 comparables over a family of related tools.

For each leading buyer write the sentence the seller says where the take-up is made (the offering is given away today, so it has a take-up, not a sale), two sentences at most, addressed to a roster profile named by its row number and the roster's own description of the person, never by name, with that person's likely answer. Where the roster holds no profile for the buyer, mark it "no profile" and address the buyer as the comparables' pages address them. The sentence is the one that would win this buyer as the evidence shows the market winning them, not a description of the program as it stands: where it says something the program does not do today, it says it, and the capability note's cost line for it travels with the parameter, since a capability not yet built is a cost and not a bound. Each sentence cites the findings, register sections and search findings it stands on.

Then list every parameter the sentences contain. A parameter is in a sentence where a different value would change what the sentence says: what the offering is called, where it is had, what one takes up, what it costs, which systems it runs on, what it does with the file, and whatever else your sentences turn on. Put each as a question in the words the buyer meets it, as `3-decisions/questions.md` puts its questions, and give for each: which of the sentences contain it; whether the market evidence suggests its options or its recommendation would differ by buyer; whether the four cards the method names as carrying a search-demand leg (name, surface, unit, season) apply to it; and the findings that show more than one way the market answers it, or the record that would show them where you find fewer than two. Give no value, option ranking or recommendation for any parameter here; the cards do that. A parameter that is in no sentence is design freedom by construction, and you leave it out; where a question in `3-decisions/questions.md` falls out this way, say so in one line.

Write one file, `3-decisions/leading-buyers.md`: a heading per buyer with its sentence, its profile line and likely answer, and its citations; then the parameter list. Your final message gives the sentences and the parameter list with, per parameter, the sentences containing it.
