**`3-decisions/card-0-one-offer-or-several.md`**: whether this is one offering or several, and for whom. Its options are the ways the corpus shows such a thing sold: as one program; as a program with its components offered beside it; as a free core with a paid tier or add-on; as a suite of related tools from one maker; and whatever else the taxonomy shows, each with its prevalence and cell, a capability leg, and a seller's sentence. The Joint line names card 1 and says what changes on this card under each buyer the owner might rule.

On both cards: options exclude one another, and doing two together is its own option with its own figure. The Recommended line follows the form to the letter: the margin in the runner-up's unit, what it rests on (the market, the operator's own buyers, or both), the instrument that saw both options and which way it leans, and withheld where the lead would be erased by one comparable coded the other way. The four boundary tests are argued between the options. Cost lines come from the capability note by name. Where the corpus, the rivals and the search findings between them show fewer than two figured options, the card is a named gap for Survey, and you say which record would close it.
