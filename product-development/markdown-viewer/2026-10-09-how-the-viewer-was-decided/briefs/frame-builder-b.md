# Frame builder B: the stores

Read `briefs/standing-brief-block.md` first, then `briefs/venue-situation.md`, then `/usr/local/src/aesop/pmf-sage/sage-S-survey.md` section 1 steps 2 and 3, then `/usr/local/src/aesop/pmf-sage/INVARIANTS.md` rule I6, then `0-comparables/shape-note.md`. Nothing else in the repository is open to you.

A frame is populated from published lists that can be re-opened and counted, standing for the market buyers choose from. Your lists, each named under the strike-list row that admits its buyer:

1. **The Mac App Store**, through Apple's search API, `https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=markdown&entity=macSoftware&limit=200`, keeping every field the response gives (name, seller, price, formatted price, user rating count, average rating, release and current-version dates, description, URL, minimum OS). Admits writers and authors (RULED strike-list:writers-and-authors), note-takers and personal knowledge managers (RULED strike-list:note-takers-and-personal-knowledge-managers) and plain readers of received markdown files (RULED strike-list:plain-readers-of-received-markdown-files).
2. **Flathub**, through `POST https://flathub.org/api/v2/search` with body `{"query":"markdown"}`, paging through every hit, keeping each app's id, name, summary and installs over the last month, and the app page's stated verification, licence and release date where the API gives them. Admits plain readers of received markdown files (RULED strike-list:plain-readers-of-received-markdown-files), note-takers and personal knowledge managers (RULED strike-list:note-takers-and-personal-knowledge-managers), writers and authors (RULED strike-list:writers-and-authors) and students and academics (RULED strike-list:students-and-academics).
3. **The Snap Store**, through `https://api.snapcraft.io/v2/snaps/find?q=markdown` with the header `Snap-Device-Series: 16`, keeping name, summary, publisher and whatever counts the API gives; say whether its 100 results are a cap. Admits the same rows as Flathub (RULED strike-list:plain-readers-of-received-markdown-files).

Eligibility, applied to every entry and recorded with the reason: an entry is in when it is an application a person installs to read or write markdown files. Out: libraries, converters, linters, static-site generators, themes, and apps whose description names markdown only as an export format of something else. Where the description cannot settle it, keep it as "undecidable" for the frame review rather than deciding by name.

Write one TSV per list under `0-comparables/frame/`: `mac-app-store.tsv`, `flathub.tsv`, `snap.tsv`, with columns: list, entry, identifier or URL, the list's own counts for the entry, price as the list states it, description as the list gives it, eligible (in, out, undecidable), reason. Write one coverage note per list beside it, `<list>-coverage.md`: who runs the list, how many entries it held when you opened it, the date, who is structurally shut out of it (a store carries only what a maker chose to list there, and a search term carries only what names or describes itself with that word), every step from list size to frame size with its count, and whether the cell ends as a census or a sample under which rule.

Your final message gives, per list, the counts list to eligible, what surprised you against what you expected, and what you could not reach.
